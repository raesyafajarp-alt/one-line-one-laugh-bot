## The entry (round 2)

> My dog judges my life choices. He's right, but he also eats his own vomit.

(74 characters, one line, no setup needed.)

## Why this line

Round 1's autocorrect line ranked 1763/1879, so round 2 replaces it with a
sharper everyday observation. The joke works on the judge's criteria, in
order: it is funny on its own (a dog as a moral judge), the turn lands fast
(the second clause undercuts the judge in one blow), the angle is fresh (the
judge's authority collapses because he eats his own vomit), the wording is
tight (every word earns its place), and it holds up on a second read and is
easy to repeat to someone else.

An earlier round-2 draft ("I deleted my social media apps to be productive.
Now I refresh my inbox like it's a slot machine.", 97 characters) was tried
first and then replaced: its punchline arrives after a setup, while the dog
line's twist lands inside the line itself.

## What the program does

`main.py` writes that single funny line to `/output/entry.txt` (UTF-8, 1–200 characters, as the competition gate requires). It asserts the length is in range and that the entry is a single line before writing.

## How to run

```sh
python3 main.py
cat /output/entry.txt
```
